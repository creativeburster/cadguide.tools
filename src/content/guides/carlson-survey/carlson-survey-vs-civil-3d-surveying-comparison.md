# Carlson Survey vs Civil 3D: Surveying Software Comparison for Land Surveyors

Both Carlson Survey and Civil 3D are used by land surveyors, but they approach surveying differently. Carlson is surveyor-focused; Civil 3D is engineer-focused with survey capabilities. I've used both for years. Here's my comparison for survey firms.

## Target Users

**Carlson Survey**: Built specifically for land surveyors. Handles field-to-finish, COGO, plat drafting, and stakeout. Runs on AutoCAD or IntelliCAD (cheaper).

**Civil 3D**: Built for civil engineers with survey tools included. Handles survey import, surface modeling, alignment design, and grading. Runs on AutoCAD only.

## Field Data Import

| Feature | Carlson Survey | Civil 3D |
|---------|---------------|----------|
| Field to Finish | Excellent (F2F module) | Good (Survey Figure Prefixes) |
| Raw data formats | 15+ formats | 10+ formats (via Survey Database) |
| Description keys | Yes (F2F file) | Yes (Description Keys) |
| Auto-linework | Excellent (F2F coding) | Good (Figure Prefixes) |
| Data collector support | Excellent (all major brands) | Good (via Survey Database) |
| Traverse adjustment | Built-in (Compass, Transit, LS) | Built-in (Compass, Transit, LS, Crandall) |

**Verdict**: Carlson's Field to Finish is more mature and flexible. Civil 3D's survey tools are adequate but less intuitive. For surveyors who process field data daily, Carlson is faster.

## COGO and Calculations

| Feature | Carlson Survey | Civil 3D |
|---------|---------------|----------|
| Inverse | Yes | Yes (via Inquiry tool) |
| Traverse | Yes | Yes (via Survey Database) |
| Intersections | Yes (BB, DD, BD) | Yes (via Survey Database) |
| Area calculation | Yes | Yes (via Surface/Areas) |
| Curve calculations | Yes | Yes (via Alignment Curves) |
| Station-offset | Yes | Yes (via Alignments) |
| Lot/parcel design | Yes (Lot by Area/Frontage) | Yes (via Parcels) |

**Verdict**: Comparable COGO capabilities. Carlson's COGO interface is more direct — enter points and get results. Civil 3D's COGO is buried in the Survey Database and Alignment tools, making it less accessible.

## Surface Modeling

| Feature | Carlson Survey | Civil 3D |
|---------|---------------|----------|
| TIN creation | Yes (Triangulate) | Yes (Surface Styles) |
| Breaklines | Yes | Yes (excellent) |
| Contours | Yes (auto-generated) | Yes (auto-generated, excellent) |
| Volume calculation | Yes (Grid, TIN) | Yes (TIN to TIN) |
| Surface editing | Yes (swap edges, delete points) | Yes (excellent — surface editing tools) |
| Surface analysis | Basic (elevations, slopes) | Excellent (elevation bands, slopes, watersheds) |

**Verdict**: Civil 3D is superior for surface modeling. The surface editing tools, analysis capabilities, and dynamic surface styles are more advanced. Carlson's surface tools are adequate for survey deliverables but lack the depth for engineering analysis.

## Plat and Drawing Production

| Feature | Carlson Survey | Civil 3D |
|---------|---------------|----------|
| Plat drafting | Excellent (dedicated plat tools) | Good (using standard AutoCAD) |
| Legal description writer | Yes (auto-generate from points) | No (manual or third-party) |
| Title block automation | Yes | Yes (via Sheet Set Manager) |
| Annotation | Yes (survey annotations) | Yes (labels and tags) |
| Parcel labels | Yes | Yes (dynamic parcel labels) |
| Drawing templates | Yes (survey templates) | Yes (civil templates) |

**Verdict**: Carlson is better for plat production. The legal description writer alone saves hours per plat. Civil 3D's parcel labels are dynamic but the overall plat workflow is more engineering-oriented.

## Construction Staking

| Feature | Carlson Survey | Civil 3D |
|---------|---------------|----------|
| Stakeout reports | Yes (built-in) | No (manual) |
| Point export for staking | Yes (all formats) | Yes (via point export) |
| Station-offset staking | Yes | Yes (via Alignment) |
| Cut/fill reports | Yes | Yes (via Volume surfaces) |

**Verdict**: Carlson has better staking tools. The stakeout report generator is purpose-built for surveyors. Civil 3D requires more manual work for staking output.

## Platform and Cost

| | Carlson Survey | Civil 3D |
|--|---------------|----------|
| CAD platform | AutoCAD OR IntelliCAD | AutoCAD only |
| License type | Perpetual or subscription | Subscription only |
| Standalone cost | ~$1,500/year (with IntelliCAD) | ~$2,800/year |
| With AutoCAD | ~$2,500/year | Included |
| Perpetual option | Yes (~$3,000 + maintenance) | No |

**Verdict**: Carlson is significantly cheaper, especially with the IntelliCAD option (no AutoCAD required). Civil 3D is subscription-only and more expensive.

## When to Choose Carlson Survey

- You're a land surveyor (not a civil engineer)
- Your primary work is boundary surveys, topographic surveys, and staking
- You need Field to Finish automation
- You need legal description generation
- You want a perpetual license option
- Budget is a concern (IntelliCAD option saves on AutoCAD cost)
- Your crew uses Carlson data collectors (seamless integration)

## When to Choose Civil 3D

- You do both surveying and civil engineering
- You need advanced surface modeling and analysis
- You design roads, grading plans, and pipe networks
- You work in a firm where engineers and surveyors share the same platform
- You need dynamic labels and objects that update automatically
- You're already in the Autodesk ecosystem (Revit, InfraWorks)

## When to Use Both

Many survey firms use both:
- **Carlson Survey** for: Field data processing, COGO, plat drafting, legal descriptions, staking
- **Civil 3D** for: Surface modeling, contour generation, volume calculations, engineering design

This requires both licenses but gives the surveyor the best tool for each task.

## My Recommendation

**For a pure surveying firm**: Carlson Survey. The survey-specific tools, Field to Finish, legal description writer, and lower cost make it the clear choice. The IntelliCAD option eliminates the AutoCAD cost entirely.

**For a combined survey/engineering firm**: Civil 3D. The shared platform between surveyors and engineers streamlines the workflow. Surveyors use the survey tools; engineers use the design tools — all in the same DWG.

**For a survey firm that also does some engineering**: Carlson Survey + Carlson Civil. The Carlson Civil module adds surface modeling and road design to Carlson Survey, creating a complete survey + light engineering package at a lower cost than Civil 3D.
