# TopSolid vs SolidWorks: Integrated CAD/CAM vs General-Purpose CAD

Users on Practical Machinist and WOODWEB forums have extensively discussed the trade-offs between TopSolid (by Missler Software, France) and SolidWorks (by Dassault Systèmes). The discussions reveal that the choice depends heavily on the industry, CAM requirements, and support needs.

## Tool Overview

| Feature | TopSolid | SolidWorks |
|---|---|---|
| Developer | Missler Software (France) | Dassault Systèmes (France/USA) |
| Geometric kernel | Parasolid | Parasolid |
| CAD/CAM integration | Native (TopSolid'Cam built-in) | Requires third-party CAM (SolidCAM, Mastercam, etc.) |
| Industry focus | Manufacturing (machining, wood, sheet metal) | General mechanical design |
| Platform | Windows | Windows |
| Global market share | Smaller (strong in France, Europe) | Large (global standard) |
| Community size | Small | Very large |
| Training resources | Limited (mostly via reseller) | Extensive (YouTube, books, courses) |
| Price | Comparable to SolidWorks + CAM add-on | SolidWorks + separate CAM cost |

## The Core Difference: Integrated vs. Separate CAM

### TopSolid: Integrated CAD/CAM
TopSolid is designed as a single system where CAD and CAM are native modules:
- The CAD model and CAM toolpaths share the same database
- Design changes automatically update toolpaths
- No data translation between CAD and CAM
- One user interface, one learning curve

A Practical Machinist user who switched from Mastercam to TopSolid stated: "Fast forward to today and here I am programming DMG DMC-80's and 125's about to bring some Hermles online with TopSolid and I couldn't be happier."

### SolidWorks: CAD + Third-Party CAM
SolidWorks is primarily a CAD system. For CAM, users add:
- **SolidCAM**: Integrated within SolidWorks (closest to TopSolid's model)
- **Mastercam**: Standalone CAM that imports SolidWorks files
- **CAMWorks**: Integrated CAM with feature recognition
- **HSMWorks**: Autodesk's integrated CAM (now Fusion 360 CAM)

This means:
- Two separate software products to license and learn
- Data translation between CAD and CAM (potential for errors)
- Design changes may require CAM re-import or reprogramming
- But: more CAM options and specialization

## Practical Machinist Forum Discussion: "Convince Me Not to Buy TopSolid"

A user considering TopSolid asked the forum for reasons NOT to buy it. Key responses:

### Support Concerns
A respondent noted: "Support is probably the main reason. Forums, etc. How is TopSolid's support and do they have as large of a forum community if you get stuck?"

TopSolid has a smaller user community than SolidWorks/Mastercam:
- Fewer online tutorials and YouTube videos
- Smaller forum community for troubleshooting
- Support is primarily through the VAR (Value Added Reseller)
- If your VAR is good, support is excellent; if not, you're stuck

### Familiarity Factor
"If you're used to how Mastercam does things, I'd advise sticking with it purely out of familiarity so you're more efficient, especially if the price is the same."

Switching CAD/CAM systems has a significant learning curve cost. If you're already productive in SolidWorks + Mastercam, switching to TopSolid may take months to regain the same efficiency.

### TopSolid Support Experience
A TopSolid user on Practical Machinist (page 5 of the "TopSolid Cam, anyone using it/loving it?" thread) stated: "I am a TopSolid fan. I have never heard of a paid promoter of TopSolid. I have been using it for a while and know the TopSolid people well. That being said, Support could use some improvement. Post edits can take some time, but they have always been able to get my post to do what is needed for zero extra cost."

### Long-Term User Experience
A V6 (older version) user on Practical Machinist: "I have used the V6 program for a long time and know it well for our uses, so I require little to no support. What support I've needed has been handled well by my VAR, who I now consider a friend. I can't speak for someone who might need lots of support initially."

## WOODWEB Forum: TopSolid for Woodworking

A WOODWEB user compared TopSolid'Wood with SolidWorks for millwork:

"The commercial company I last worked with recently bought SolidWorks since some of their engineers had experience with that software but it seems like after talking to them that it just isn't as easy to build and engineer millwork products in it. In TopSolid, I can easily make fully parametric products."

Key points for woodworking:
- **TopSolid'Wood**: Purpose-built for wood — tenons, grooves, edge banding, nesting, wood-specific CAM
- **SolidWorks**: General-purpose CAD — can design furniture but requires workarounds for wood-specific features
- **TopSolid'Wood**: Integrated CAD/CAM — design and machining in one system
- **SolidWorks + SWood**: SWood is a SolidWorks add-in for woodworking, but it's an additional cost and not as deeply integrated

## SolidCAM Forum: SolidCAM vs TopSolid

A SolidCAM forum post compared SolidCAM + SolidWorks vs TopSolid for moldmaking:
- Both use the Parasolid kernel
- SolidCAM integrates within SolidWorks (similar to TopSolid's integrated approach)
- TopSolid has a longer history in moldmaking (French mold industry)
- SolidCAM has a larger user base globally

## Feature Comparison by Industry

### General Mechanical Design
- **SolidWorks**: Industry standard, extensive community, better for general mechanical design
- **TopSolid**: Capable but smaller community, better when CAM is needed

### CNC Machining / Moldmaking
- **TopSolid**: Integrated CAD/CAM, strong in mold and tooling, 5-axis support
- **SolidWorks + Mastercam**: More flexible CAM options, larger CAM community
- **SolidWorks + SolidCAM**: Closest to TopSolid's integrated model

### Woodworking / Furniture
- **TopSolid'Wood**: Purpose-built for wood, no real competitor in integrated wood CAD/CAM
- **SolidWorks + SWood**: Add-in approach, less integrated
- **SolidWorks + separate CAM**: Requires manual CAM programming for wood operations

### Sheet Metal
- **TopSolid**: Integrated sheet metal design and manufacturing
- **SolidWorks**: Strong sheet metal design, separate CAM for manufacturing

## When to Choose TopSolid

- Integrated CAD/CAM is a priority (one system, one license, one learning curve)
- Woodworking/furniture is your primary industry
- Mold and tool manufacturing is your focus
- You're in France/Europe where TopSolid has strong reseller support
- You want design changes to automatically update CAM toolpaths
- You're starting fresh (no existing CAD/CAM investment)

## When to Choose SolidWorks

- General mechanical design is your primary need
- Large user community and extensive learning resources are important
- You need compatibility with a global supply chain (most partners use SolidWorks)
- You already have a CAM solution (Mastercam, SolidCAM, CAMWorks)
- Industry certification (CSWA/CSWP) is valuable for hiring
- You need integration with analysis tools (SIMULIA, ANSYS)
- You're in a market where SolidWorks is the standard

## Cost Considerations

- **TopSolid**: Single license includes CAD + CAM (pricing via reseller)
- **SolidWorks + CAM**: SolidWorks license + separate CAM license (often more expensive combined)
- **Training cost**: TopSolid training is via reseller; SolidWorks has many free/low-cost online resources
- **Support cost**: Both have annual maintenance/support fees

For a company that needs both CAD and CAM, TopSolid's integrated pricing may be more cost-effective than SolidWorks + separate CAM. However, the smaller community means more reliance on paid support.
