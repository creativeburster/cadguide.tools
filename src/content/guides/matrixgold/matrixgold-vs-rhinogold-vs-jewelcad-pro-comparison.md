# MatrixGold vs RhinoGold vs JewelCAD Pro: Jewelry CAD Software Comparison

We've worked with all three of these jewelry CAD tools in different production environments. MatrixGold, RhinoGold, and JewelCAD Pro each approach jewelry design differently — MatrixGold is a parametric builder system on Rhino, RhinoGold is a simpler Rhino plugin, and JewelCAD Pro is a standalone jewelry CAD with its own modeling kernel. Understanding their differences helps jewelers choose the right tool for their specific workflow.

## Quick Comparison

| Feature | MatrixGold | RhinoGold | JewelCAD Pro |
|---------|-----------|-----------|-------------|
| Platform | Rhino plugin | Rhino plugin | Standalone |
| Modeling approach | Parametric builders | Manual + tools | Surface modeling |
| Gem library | Yes (extensive) | Yes | Yes |

| Ring builders | Yes (parametric) | Yes (basic) | Yes |

## MatrixGold

### Strengths

- **Parametric builder system**: Ring rails, profile placers, and prong placers update automatically when parameters change
- **Automated gem setting**: Prong Placer, Gem Cutter, Channel Cutter, and pave tools automate repetitive setting tasks
- **Extensive gem library**: Accurate gem models with proper cuts and proportions
- **Dynamic commands**: Mirror, array, and sweep tools that maintain parametric relationships
- **Production reports**: Metal weight, gem report, and tech report generation
- **Built on Rhino**: Full access to Rhino's surface modeling tools alongside jewelry-specific tools
- **KeyShot rendering**: Integrated rendering for photorealistic metal and gem visualization

### Weaknesses

- Requires Rhino license (additional cost)
- Learning curve for the builder system and dynamic commands
- Some advanced tools require understanding of Rhino's surface modeling
- Not standalone — depends on Rhino version compatibility

### Best For

- Production jewelry designers who need automated gem setting
- Jewelers transitioning from manual techniques to CAD
- Designers who need parametric control for iterative design
- Jewelry companies that need production reports and documentation

The MatrixGold training guide states: "MatrixGold has been developed specially for jewelry designing and is very easy to learn and work with. Also, it is the one of the best computer-aided-design programs for jewelry design that is being taught as the primary software in well-known universities and institutes such as GIA in USA."

## RhinoGold

### Strengths

- **Easier to learn** than MatrixGold — simpler interface and fewer tools
- **Built on Rhino**: Full Rhino surface modeling capability
- **Basic jewelry tools**: Ring builders, gem placement, simple prong tools
- **Lower cost** than MatrixGold
- **Good for simple designs**: Solitaire rings, basic pendants, simple earrings

### Weaknesses

- Fewer automated tools than MatrixGold
- No advanced pave or channel cutting tools
- Limited parametric control — changes require manual updates
- Less comprehensive gem library
- No built-in production reports
- Development has slowed (fewer updates in recent years)

### Best For

- Hobbyist jewelers and small studios
- Designers who primarily do simple jewelry (solitaires, basic bands)
- Users who want Rhino's modeling power with basic jewelry tools
- Budget-conscious buyers who don't need advanced automation

## JewelCAD Pro

### Strengths

- **Standalone software**: No Rhino dependency
- **Surface modeling kernel**: Designed specifically for jewelry, not a plugin
- **Good curve tools**: Strong surface modeling for organic jewelry shapes
- **Gem library**: Includes standard gem cuts and sizes
- **Rendering**: Built-in rendering for client presentations
- **STL export**: Direct export for 3D printing and casting
- **Popular in Asia**: Widely used in Asian jewelry manufacturing

### Weaknesses

- Not based on Rhino — different modeling paradigm
- Smaller user community in Western markets
- Fewer third-party tutorials and training resources
- Less automated gem setting than MatrixGold
- Limited parametric control
- Interface can feel dated compared to Rhino-based tools

### Best For

- Jewelers who don't want to learn Rhino
- Asian manufacturing workflows
- Designers who prefer surface modeling over parametric builders
- Studios that need standalone software without plugin dependencies

## Feature-by-Feature Comparison

### Ring Design

- **MatrixGold**: Parametric ring rails with automatic size adjustment. Change ring size and the entire ring updates. Profile Placer for custom shank shapes. Cathedral and split shank builders.
- **RhinoGold**: Basic ring builder with limited parametric control. Size changes require manual adjustment. Fewer shank style options.
- **JewelCAD Pro**: Surface-based ring modeling. Good for custom shapes but less automated. No parametric ring sizing.

### Gem Setting

- **MatrixGold**: Fully automated. Prong Placer generates prongs at correct positions. Gem Cutter cuts seats. Channel Cutter creates channels. Pave patterns with bead generation.
- **RhinoGold**: Semi-automated. Basic prong placement. Manual gem seat cutting. Limited pave support.
- **JewelCAD Pro**: Mostly manual. Gem placement tools exist but prong and seat creation is manual. No automated channel cutting.

### Pave Setting

- **MatrixGold**: Gem on Surface with grid/hexagonal patterns. Automatic bead generation with Prong Placer. Multi-row pave with shared beads.
- **RhinoGold**: Limited pave tools. Manual bead placement. No automated pattern generation.
- **JewelCAD Pro**: Manual gem placement in patterns. No automated bead generation. Pave requires significant manual work.

### Rendering

- **MatrixGold**: Integrated KeyShot rendering with metal and gem materials. Photorealistic results. Animation support.
- **RhinoGold**: Basic rendering with limited material options. Not photorealistic.
- **JewelCAD Pro**: Built-in rendering with basic materials. Adequate for client review but not photorealistic.

### Production Documentation

- **MatrixGold**: Metal weight report, gem report, tech report with dimensions and views. Complete production documentation.
- **RhinoGold**: Basic measurements. No automated reports.
- **JewelCAD Pro**: Basic reports. Gem list and measurements. Less comprehensive than MatrixGold.

### 3D Printing Export

- **MatrixGold**: STL export with mesh density control. Show Edges for naked edge detection. Boolean Union for single solid verification.
- **RhinoGold**: STL export through Rhino. Same mesh tools as Rhino.
- **JewelCAD Pro**: Direct STL export. Basic mesh checking tools.

## Which Should You Choose?

### Choose MatrixGold If:
- You design **production jewelry** with gem settings
- You need **automated pave, channel, and prong tools**
- You want **parametric control** for iterative design
- You need **production reports** for casting and gem ordering
- You're willing to learn Rhino as the base platform
- You teach at or attend a jewelry school (GIA and others use MatrixGold)

### Choose RhinoGold If:
- You're a **hobbyist or small studio** with simple designs
- You want **Rhino's modeling power** with basic jewelry tools
- You have a **limited budget**
- You primarily make **solitaires and simple bands**
- You don't need automated gem setting tools

### Choose JewelCAD Pro If:
- You don't want to **learn or license Rhino**
- You prefer **standalone software**
- You work in **Asian manufacturing** where JewelCAD is standard
- You like **surface modeling** for organic jewelry shapes
- You don't need advanced parametric builders

## Summary

MatrixGold, RhinoGold, and JewelCAD Pro serve different segments of the jewelry CAD market. MatrixGold is the most capable — its parametric builder system, automated gem setting tools, and production reports make it ideal for production jewelers who need efficiency and documentation. RhinoGold is simpler and cheaper, suitable for hobbyists and small studios with basic design needs. JewelCAD Pro is a standalone alternative popular in Asian markets, offering surface modeling without Rhino dependency. For most professional jewelry designers in Western markets, MatrixGold offers the best combination of automation, parametric control, and production documentation. The choice ultimately depends on your design complexity, budget, and whether you already use or want to learn Rhino.

When evaluating these tools, focus on the features that matter most for your specific workflow: automated gem setting for production speed, parametric builders for iterative design, rendering quality for client presentations, and STL export reliability for casting. Each tool has strengths in different areas, and the best choice is the one that aligns with your production volume, design complexity, and team capabilities.
