Title: Top 5 AutoCAD Alternatives with Full AutoLISP Support (2026 Pricing Included)

The #1 fear when leaving AutoCAD isn't the interface — it's the LISP routines. Years of accumulated .lsp files, custom commands, and automation scripts are the real switching cost. AutoCAD itself is now subscription-only at $255/month ($2,030/year), and every one of the alternatives below still runs your existing AutoLISP code.

Here are the five worth a pilot test, with 2026 list pricing.

## 1. GstarCAD — $499 one-time (perpetual)

The strongest "total cost" story. A perpetual license costs less than three months of AutoCAD, it reads and writes DWG natively, and AutoLISP support is built in — most firms migrate their routines without a single rewrite. Its drawing engine is also notably fast on large 2D files. Windows only.

## 2. ZWCAD — from $899

ZWCAD's Smart Mouse and voice annotations divide opinion, but the core is solid: native DWG, AutoLISP compatibility, and both subscription and perpetual licensing options. Widely adopted in architecture and interior design workflows.

## 3. BricsCAD — from $590/year

The pick if your roadmap includes 3D or BIM. BricsCAD's LISP engine is one of the most complete third-party implementations, and it scales from Lite (2D) through Pro (3D modeling) to Ultimate (BIM). Subscription-based, with volume pricing for teams.

## 4. progeCAD — $399 one-time

The budget perpetual option. progeCAD targets exactly the AutoCAD-replacement use case: AutoLISP, native DWG, familiar command set. Fewer bells and whistles than BricsCAD, but the price-to-capability ratio is hard to argue with.

## 5. DraftSight — $299/year

From Dassault Systèmes, so it carries real corporate pedigree. Professional tier re-introduced LISP support (the completely-free era of DraftSight is over, but at $299/year it undercuts AutoCAD by nearly 90%). Best for teams already in the Dassault orbit.

## Bonus: nanoCAD

Has a free tier for evaluation, with LISP available in its commercial editions. Worth testing if your budget is literally zero today.

## How to choose

All five run AutoLISP, so the decision comes down to: perpetual vs subscription, 2D vs 3D roadmap, and total seats × years. Before you commit, compare them side by side on pricing, licensing, platforms, and API support — we keep a free, no-signup comparison of these tools at CADGuide.tools.

Whatever you pick: pilot with your real LISP routines and your messiest real drawing. The demo files always work; it's the production files that vote.
